Indoor AMR, 30 kg class
Design proposal

A steered-hub-wheel AMR in an H layout, built on a welded mild-steel skeleton with bolt-on shells and a full-perimeter LiDAR band. It is sized to your 32 × 23 × 12 in envelope and built from the parts in AGV Budget Plan.xlsx.

Envelope (L × W × H)813 × 584 × 305 mm
Payload (rated / frame)30 kg / 60 kg
Robot mass (est.)≈ 46 kg
Drive2 × M0601, stepper-steered
Top speed (14.8 V)≈ 0.5 m/s
LiPo runtime (driving)≈ 1.8 h

Left elevation, to scale. Lower bay (40–185 mm), open LiDAR band (185–240 mm, only the 4 corner posts cross it) and upper section with deck (240–305 mm). The front upper face is sloped; the Pi 5 port cover (P) sits on the left wall.

What this proposal asks you to decide

  1. Casters: 100 mm (4 in) low-profile casters to match the 100.7 mm drive wheel, instead of 6 in (§1).
  2. Battery for auto-docking: the LiPo packs need a BMS before the dock can charge them unattended (§6).
  3. Payload: the BOM is sized for 20–30 kg. A higher payload needs a different drive motor (§9).

4MS skeleton, shells and printed parts

Everything bolts to the skeleton: motor carriages, casters, trays, posts, shells and the deck. The skeleton is one welded mild-steel frame made of three rings joined by four corner posts. It is powder-coated after welding and fitted with M5 rivet nuts so every other part can be removed with a hex key.

Joining rules

  • Weld on a flat jig plate. Tack all 4 posts, check diagonals (≤ 2 mm difference), then finish the welds.
  • Drill and fit rivet nuts after powder coat: M5 every ≤ 150 mm on the shell lines, M4 on the trays.
  • Caster plates are machined flat after welding. Use shims to level the frame to the drive-wheel ride height.
  • Guide rods are clamped top and bottom (bottom cross-member → mid cross-member), so the rods also stiffen the frame.

The Pi 5 lies flat on standoffs at z ≈ 120 mm with its USB/Ethernet edge against the left wall. A 64 × 30 mm window exposes Ethernet and the 2 USB 2.0 ports for service. 6Auto-docking and charging

Robot side (female)

  • 2 × 8 mm brass sockets, recessed 8 mm (finger-safe), 80 mm apart, z = 92 mm, inside a PETG funnel with a 30° lead-in.
  • Charge path: socket → Schottky/ideal diode → 15 A fuse → BMS charge port. A voltage divider tells the Mega when charging has started.
  • Rear webcam centred at z ≈ 259 mm, looking straight back.

Dock side (male)

  • Floor plate with a V-guide, a 350 mm tower and 2 × Ø8 brass pins on 10 mm spring compliance.
  • ArUco DICT_4X4_50, id 0, 120 mm, centred at z = 259 mm so it is level with the rear camera.
  • 16.8 V 5 A CC/CV charger. The pins are dead until a robot is sensed: a small dock MCU checks for an ID resistor across the pins at low current, then closes a relay.

Docking sequence

  1. Navigate (map) to a pre-dock pose 1.0 m in front of the dock, then rotate so the rear faces the dock.
  2. The rear camera finds the ArUco marker. Solve the marker pose with solvePnP.
  3. Visual servo while reversing at 0.05 m/s. The steered wheels allow lateral crab correction without turning the body.
  4. Stop when the charge-detect line goes high (or after a 30 mm over-travel limit). Drive power off.
  5. The dock relay closes and the BMS charges. LED bars breathe blue, then show solid green when full.
  6. To undock: drive forward 0.3 m, then resume the mission.
Battery decision. The BOM uses 2 × Wild Scorpion 4S 4200 mAh LiPo with an IMAX B6 bench charger. That is fine for Phase 1 with manual charging off the robot. For unattended dock charging, either fit a 4S BMS with balancing to each LiPo, or (recommended) switch to 2 × 4S Li-ion packs with a built-in BMS. 4S3P 21700 packs give about 2 × 170 Wh against 2 × 62 Wh today, and runtime rises from about 1.8 h to about 5 h. The bays (180 × 80 × 75 mm) are sized to take either.

7Light bars — front and back, both sides

Four WS2812B bars cut from the BOM 30 LED/m strip, one at each corner: front-left, front-right, rear-left, rear-right. Each bar wraps from the chamfer 100 mm onto the side, so it is visible from the front/back and from the side, like the reference robot. Front bars have 7 LEDs and rear bars 6 (14 + 12 = 26 LEDs, about 1 A at full white). They are chained on 1 Mega pin (330 Ω series resistor, 1000 µF capacitor at the strip) and powered by their own 5 V 3 A buck, with brightness capped at 40% (≈ 1.5 A max).

Both use 80% of the stored energy. The TB6600 has no automatic idle-current reduction: at 2.8 A the 2 steppers alone would draw about 28 W. Set the drivers to 2.0 A and drop ENA whenever the wheel angle is not changing; the belt friction holds the angle. RS485: both M0601s share one bus (IDs 1 and 2), with 120 Ω termination at the far motor and the twisted pair kept away from the stepper cables.
